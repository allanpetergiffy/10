Explanation of the Ten Commandments

PROLOGUE

Three things are necessary for man to be saved: (1) knowledge of what is to be believed, (2) knowledge of what is to be desired, and (3) knowledge of what is to be done.

The first is taught in the Creed, where knowledge of the articles of faith is given; the second is in the Lord's Prayer; the third is in the Law.

A Fourfold Law

Here we are concerned with knowledge of what is to be done, and with regard to this there is a fourfold law:

(1) The first is the law of nature, and that is nothing other than the light of the intellect planted in us by God, by which we know what should be done and what should be avoided. God gave this to all men that they might know what they should do.

(2) Because the law of nature was destroyed by the law of concupiscence, man needed to be brought back to the works of virtue and drawn away from vice, and for that the law of Scripture was needed.

(3) But because that method was insufficient, and the law given by Moses which drew people from evil by fear was insufficient, in that it restrained the hand but did not restrain desire, there was a need for another law—the law of grace.

(4) The fourth law is the law of divine love, which transforms us internally and makes us willing to do the good.

Four Effects of Charity

At this point note that this law of divine love produces four very desirable effects in man: (1) First it causes spiritual life in him. For it is clear that what is loved is inside the lover. And so, being loved by God and loving Him in return, we are united with Him and have life through Him.

The second effect of charity is the observance of the divine commandments. Gregory says: "The love of God is never lazy. It does great things if it is there; if it refuses to work it is not love."

The third effect of charity is to be a bulwark against adversity. For no adversity hurts someone who has charity, but it is all converted into good use: "For those who love God, everything works together for good."

The fourth effect of charity is that it leads to happiness. For eternal happiness is promised only to those who have charity.

How to Get and Increase Charity

So much for the benefits of charity. It now remains to work hard to acquire it and hold onto it. But we must realize that no one can possess charity on his own, but it is the gift of God alone.

In spite of the fact that charity is a divine gift, to possess it requires a disposition on our part. There are two requisites for acquiring charity: (1) The first is the removal of obstacles, namely, the mortification of carnal desires.

(2) The second requisite is continual thinking about good things: "My heart became hot within me." So if you want to acquire divine love, meditate on good things.

ARTICLE 1: LOVE OF GOD

Before his passion, the doctors of the Law asked Christ which was the greatest and first commandment. He said: "Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul and with all your mind."

"Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind. This is the greatest and the first commandment."

But to fulfill this commandment of love perfectly, four things are required. The first is the recollection of the divine benefits, because all that we have, whether our soul or body or external goods, comes from God's generosity.

The second is consideration of the divine excellence. For God is greater than our hearts; so if we serve him with our whole heart and strength we still fall short.

The third is renunciation of worldly and earthly things. For it is a big offense against God to equate him with anything else.

The fourth is complete avoidance of sin. For no one can love God when he is living in sin. You cannot serve God and mammon. So, if you are living in sin, you do not love God.

What Man Owes to God

It has now been shown that man must give himself to God. Now we have to see what is in him that he owes to God. Man owes God four things: his heart, his soul, his mind and his strength.

A good intention alone is not enough. There must also be a good will. For it often happens that someone acts with a good intention, but to no avail because his will is not steadfast.

But sometimes a good intention and good will are present, but there is some sin in the intellect. Therefore the whole intellect must be given to God, so that all our thoughts are in harmony with His truth.

But that is not enough. One must give God all one's power and strength. For there are some who use their strength to sin, thereby destroying all the good that comes from intention, will, and mind.

ARTICLE 2: LOVE OF NEIGHBOR

When Christ was asked which is the greatest commandment, he gave two answers to the one question. The first was "You shall love the Lord your God," which we have talked about. The second is equally important: "You shall love your neighbor as yourself."

"You shall love your neighbor as yourself. There is no commandment greater than these two."

Four Motives for Loving Our Neighbor

There are four motives for loving our neighbor: The first is divine love, since it is said: "If anyone says that he loves God, while he hates his brother, he is a liar."

The second motive is the divine precept. For when Christ was going away, he stressed this commandment to his disciples above all other commandments: "This is my commandment: that you love one another."

The third motive is our sharing in the same nature, as it is said: "Every animal loves its like." Since all men are alike in nature, they should love one another.

The fourth motive is the advantages it brings. For everything that one person has is useful to another through charity. For this is what unites the Church and makes everything common.

Five Requisites in Loving Our Neighbor

"Love your neighbor as yourself." That is the second commandment of the Law. Now we must turn to the manner in which this love should be given.

The first is that we must love him really as ourselves. We do this if we love him for his own sake, not because of our own interest.

The second point is that we must love ordinately, that is, we must not love him above God or as much as God, but along with him and for his sake.

The third point is that we must love our neighbor in practice. For you do not only love yourself, but you also take care to provide for yourself and avoid evil. You must do the same for your neighbor.

The fourth point is that we must persevere in loving our neighbor, just as you persevere in loving yourself. A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.

The fifth point is that we must love with justice and holiness, so that we do not love to bring him to sin.

I

THE FIRST COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Have Strange Gods Before Me"

The entire law of Christ depends upon charity. And charity depends on two precepts, one of which concerns loving God and the other concerns loving our neighbor. Now God, in delivering the law to Moses, gave him Ten Commandments written upon two tablets of stone. Three of these Commandments that were written on the first tablet referred to the love of God; the remaining seven referred to the love of neighbor.

Why Adore One God?

"You shall not have strange gods before Me." The First Commandment forbids us to worship other than the one God. We shall now consider five reasons for this.

God's Dignity: The first reason is the dignity of God which, were it belittled in any way, would be an injury to God. Reverence is given to those in authority.

God's Bounty: We receive every good from God; and this also is of the dignity of God, that He is the maker and giver of all good things.

The Strength of Our Promise: The third reason is taken from our solemn promise. For we have renounced the devil, and we have promised fidelity to God alone.

Against Service of the Devil: The fourth reason is because of the great burden imposed by service to the devil.

Greatness of the Reward: The fifth reason is taken from the greatness of the reward. In no law are such rewards promised as in the law of Christ.

II

THE SECOND COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Take the Name of the Lord your God in Vain"

This is the Second Commandment of the law. Just as there is but one God whom we must worship, so there is only one God whom we should reverence in a special manner. This Commandment teaches us to reverence the name of God.

The Meaning of "In Vain"

"In vain" has a threefold meaning. Sometimes it is said of that which is false. One, therefore, takes the name of God in vain when he swears falsely by the name of God.

It is an insult to God because, when you swear by God, it is nothing other than to call Him to witness; and when you swear falsely, you either believe God to be ignorant of the truth and thus deceivable, or you believe Him unwilling to punish the offender.

Likewise, such a one does an injury to himself, for he binds himself to the judgment of God. It is the same thing to say, "By God this is so," as to say, "May God punish me if it is not so."

He, finally, does an injury to other men. For there can be no lasting society unless men believe one another.

Conditions of a Lawful Oath

One cannot, therefore, swear to a falsehood, or without good reason, or in any way against justice. An oath must be taken in truth, in judgment, and in justice.

Sometimes "vain" also means foolish. Accordingly, he who takes the name of God foolishly, by blasphemy, takes His name in vain and offends grievously against this Commandment.

III

THE THIRD COMMANDMENT

"Remember to Keep Holy the Sabbath Day"

This is the Third Commandment of the law, and very suitably is it so. For we are first commanded to adore God in our hearts, and the Commandment is to worship one God. Then we are commanded to reverence His name. Finally, we are commanded to set apart one day to worship Him specially.

Reasons for This Commandment

There are five reasons for this Commandment. The first reason was to put aside error, for the Holy Spirit saw that in the future some men would say that the world had always existed. But the Sabbath was kept to commemorate the creation.

The second reason for this Commandment is to instruct us in our faith in the Redeemer. For the flesh of Christ was not corrupted in the sepulchre.

The third reason is that this Commandment was given to strengthen and foreshadow the fulfillment of the promise of rest.

Fourthly, this Commandment was given for the increase of our love, for the corruptible body is a load upon the soul.

Lastly, we are given this Commandment in order to exercise works of kindliness to those who are subject to us.

What Should We Do on the Sabbath?

"Remember that you keep holy (sanctify) the Sabbath day." We have already said that, as the Jews celebrated the Sabbath, so do we Christians observe the Sunday and all principal feasts.

The Offering of Sacrifice: The first is the offering of sacrifices. In the Book of Numbers it is written how God ordered that on each day there be offered one lamb in the morning and a lamb in the evening.

Hearing of God's Word: Our second duty on the Sabbath is to be eager to hear the word of God. This the Jews did daily: "The voices of the prophets which are read every Sabbath."

IV

THE FOURTH COMMANDMENT

"Honor your father and your mother"

Perfection for man consists in the love of God and of neighbor. Now, the three Commandments which were written on the first tablet pertain to the love of God; for the love of neighbor there remain seven Commandments on the second tablet.

The Philosopher also gives another reason for this honor to parents, in that we cannot make an equal return to our parents for the great benefits they have granted to us; and, therefore, an honor done to them should endure without end.

What Children Owe Parents

Now, because we owe our birth to our parents, we ought to honor them more than any other superiors, because from such we receive only temporal things.

Again, since we receive nourishment from our parents in our childhood, we must support them in their old age.

We must obey our parents, for they have instructed us. "Children, obey your parents in all things." This excepts, of course, those things which are contrary to God.

Rewards for Keeping This Commandment

"Honor your father and your mother." Among all the Commandments, this one only has the additional words: "that you may be long-lived upon the land." The reason for this is evident.

Grace and Glory: The first reward is grace for the present life, and glory in the life to come.

A Long Life: The second reward is a long life. For "he who honors his father shall enjoy a long life."

Grateful Children: The third reward is to have in turn grateful and pleasing children.

V

THE FIFTH COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Kill"

In the divine law which tells us we must love God and our neighbor, it is commanded that we not only do good but also avoid evil. The greatest evil that can be done to one's neighbor is to deprive him of life.

The Sin of Anger

Why We Are Forbidden to Be Angry.—In the Gospel of St. Matthew, Christ taught that our justice should be greater than the justice of the Old Law. This means that Christians should observe not only external conduct but also interior disposition.

The Lord mentioned this Commandment in particular among the others when He said: "You have heard that it was said to them of old: You shall not kill.... But I say to you that anyone who is angry with his brother shall be in danger of judgment."

Ways of Avoiding Anger: Now, there are five ways to avoid being angry. The first is that one be not quickly provoked to anger: "Let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak and slow to anger."

One should also take care that one does not remain angry over long: "Be ye angry, and sin not" and "Let not the sun go down upon your anger."

We should beware lest our anger grow in intensity, having its beginning in the heart, and finally leading on to hatred.

VI

THE SIXTH COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Commit Adultery"

After the prohibition of murder, adultery is forbidden. This is fitting, since husband and wife are as one body. "They shall be," says the Lord, "two in one flesh."

Adultery is forbidden both to the wife and the husband. The wife who commits adultery is guilty of multiple sins and transgressions against her sacred vows.

Why Adultery and Fornication Must Be Avoided

Thus, God forbids adultery both to men and women. Adultery and fornication are forbidden for a number of reasons. First of all, because they destroy the soul.

The Commandment, "You shall not commit adultery," not only forbids adultery but also every form of immodesty and impurity.

VII

THE SEVENTH COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Steal"

The Lord specifically forbids injury to our neighbor in the Commandments. Thus, "You shall not kill" forbids us to injure our neighbor in his own person; "You shall not commit adultery" forbids us to injure his family; "You shall not steal" forbids us to injure his possessions.

Why Stealing Must Be Avoided

"You shall not steal." This Commandment forbids taking things wrongfully, and we can bring forth many reasons why it is given. The first is because of the gravity of the sin.

The second reason is the peculiar danger involved in theft, for no sin is so dangerous.

The third reason is the uselessness of stolen goods in that they are of no spiritual value.

The fourth reason is that the results of theft are peculiarly harmful to the thief in that they lead to his loss of other goods.

VIII

THE EIGHTH COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Bear False Witness Against your Neighbor"

The Lord has forbidden anyone to injure his neighbor by deed; now he forbids us to injure him by word. "You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor."

Special Effects of Telling Lies

The prohibition of this Commandment includes every form of falsehood. There are four reasons for this.

The first reason is that God is the God of truth, and therefore lying is an offense against Him.

The second reason is that lying induces the ruin of society. Men live together in society, and this is soon rendered impossible if they do not speak the truth to one another.

The third reason is that the liar loses his reputation for the truth. He who is accustomed to telling lies is not believed even when he speaks the truth.

The fourth reason is because a liar kills his soul, for "the mouth that lies kills the soul."

IX

THE NINTH COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Covet your Neighbor's Goods"

"You shall not covet your neighbor's goods." There is this difference between the divine and the human laws that human law judges only deeds and words, whereas the divine law judges also thoughts and desires.

Why This Commandment Is Given

The first reason for the Commandment is that man's desire has no limits, because desire itself is boundless. But he who is wise will aim at some particular end.

The second reason is that covetousness destroys peace of heart, which is indeed highly delightful. The covetous man is ever solicitous to acquire what he lacks, and to hold that which he has.

Thirdly, covetousness in a man of wealth renders his riches useless both to himself and to others.

Finally, covetousness produces all kinds of wickedness. It is "the root of all evil," says St. Paul.

X

THE TENTH COMMANDMENT

"You Shall Not Covet your Neighbor's Wife"

St. John says in his first Epistle that "all that is in the world is the concupiscence of the flesh, the concupiscence of the eyes, and the pride of life." Now, all that is desired as evil comes under these three categories.

Because of the corruption which resulted from the Fall, none has been free from concupiscence except Christ and the glorious Virgin.

Ways to Overcome Concupiscence

We must realize that the avoidance of concupiscence demands much labor, for it is based on something within us.

Firstly, by fleeing the external occasions such as, for instance, bad company.

The second way is by not giving an opening to thoughts which of themselves are the occasion of lustful desires, and this must be done by mortification of the flesh.

The third way is perseverance in prayer.

The fourth way is to keep oneself busy with wholesome occupations.

SUMMARY OF THE TEN COMMANDMENTS

These are the ten precepts to which Our Lord referred when He said: "If you would enter into life, keep the commandments." There are two main principles of all the Commandments: love of God and love of neighbor.

But to love God worthily, one must first of all love one's neighbor. And so: "Honor your father and mother." Then, one must avoid doing harm to one's neighbor in act. "You shall not kill; You shall not commit adultery; You shall not steal." And then one must avoid doing him harm by word or desire. "You shall not bear false witness; You shall not covet."

These are the Ten Commandments, and they contain all that is necessary for the salvation of man.
